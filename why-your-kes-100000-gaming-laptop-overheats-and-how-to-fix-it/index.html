Why Your Gaming Laptop Overheats (And How to Fix It)

Editorial September 22, 2026 Opinion

You finally did it. You saved up, walked into a shop in Nairobi, and dropped KES 100,000 on a serious gaming laptop. It has an RTX graphics card, a high refresh rate screen, and enough RGB lights to host a concert.

You take it home, fire up your favorite game, and ten minutes later, the frame rate crashes. The keyboard gets uncomfortably hot. The fans sound like a jet engine taking off at JKIA.

You did not get scammed. The laptop is not broken. You have just run into the harsh reality of gaming in Nairobi: the environment is fighting back.

Here is why your powerful machine is struggling, and exactly how to fix it.

Why It Overheats in Kenya

Gaming laptops are built to push huge amounts of power through tiny spaces. That generates a lot of heat. To survive, they rely on pulling in cool air from the room and blowing hot air out.

In Kenya, three things work against this design:

1. The Dust
Nairobi is a dusty city. If your laptop sits on a desk near an open window, or if you use it on your bed, it acts like a vacuum cleaner. Within a few months, a thick layer of dust coats the internal fans and blocks the vents. Air cannot get in, and heat cannot get out.

2. The Room Temperature
Most gaming laptops are tested in air-conditioned rooms at 20°C. Unless you are gaming in an air-conditioned office in Westlands, your room might be 28°C or hotter. If the air going into the laptop is already hot, the cooling system has to work twice as hard to keep the machine from melting.

3. The Surface You Use It On
If you game on your bed, a cushion, or a wooden desk, you are suffocating the laptop. The intake vents are usually on the bottom. If they are blocked by soft fabric or flat wood, the fans spin but no air moves.

How to Fix It (Without Spending a Fortune)

You do not need to buy a new laptop. You just need to manage the environment.

1. Elevate the Laptop
This is the cheapest and most effective fix. Get a laptop stand. You can buy a basic aluminum stand on Jumia for about KES 1,500. It lifts the back of the laptop up, allowing air to flow freely underneath. If you do not have a stand, use two books to prop up the back corners. Never game with the laptop flat on a bed or blanket.

2. Clean the Fans
If you have had the laptop for more than six months, it needs cleaning. If you are comfortable with a screwdriver, you can open the back panel and use a soft brush and a can of compressed air to blow the dust out of the heatsinks. If you are not confident, take it to a repair shop in town. A basic cleaning costs about KES 1,000 to KES 2,000, and it will drop your temperatures by up to 10 degrees instantly.

3. Undervolt Your CPU
This sounds technical, but it is just a software tweak. "Undervolting" means you are lowering the voltage the processor uses. Less voltage means less heat, but you do not lose any performance. You can use free programs like ThrottleStop or Intel XTU to do this. There are hundreds of tutorials on YouTube. It is safe, and it is the secret to keeping gaming laptops cool.

4. Change Your Power Settings
Windows has a bad habit of prioritizing maximum performance over temperature. When you unplug your laptop, it limits the power to save battery. Plug it in, and it runs full blast.

Go to your Windows power settings and make sure you are using a balanced plan. For gaming, you need it plugged in (gaming laptops always do), but ensure the laptop's internal software (like Lenovo Vantage or HP Omen Gaming Hub) is set to "Performance" mode so the fans run at maximum speed while you play.

5. Repaste the CPU and GPU (Advanced)
If the laptop is more than two years old, the thermal paste (the grey substance between the processor and the cooling pipes) might have dried out. Replacing it with high-quality thermal paste is the ultimate fix, but it requires opening the laptop deep enough to remove the cooling system. Unless you know what you are doing, pay a professional to do it.

The Verdict

A KES 100,000 gaming laptop is a fantastic machine, but it needs help to survive the Kenyan climate. You cannot control the heat outside, but you can control the dust inside and the way the laptop breathes.

Keep it elevated. Clean it twice a year. Use it on a hard desk, not on a mattress. Do those three things, and your laptop will run cool, fast, and quiet for years.

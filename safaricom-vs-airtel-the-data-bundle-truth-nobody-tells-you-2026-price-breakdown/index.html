Safaricom vs Airtel: The Data Bundle Truth Nobody Talks About (2026 Price Breakdown)

Lina Maya October 21, 2026 Comparisons

If you buy data in small amounts, the last few months have been a quiet disaster. Nobody sent you an SMS announcing a price increase. The shilling figure on your bundle stayed the same. But the gigabytes you get for that money shrank, and in some cases the hours you are allowed to use them were cut.

This is the part of the Safaricom versus Airtel debate that the advertisements do not explain. Both networks have been adjusting their bundles throughout 2026, and the changes have hit different buyers in opposite ways. If you buy a KES 1,000 monthly bundle, you are probably better off today than you were six months ago. If you survive on KES 20 or KES 50 daily bundles, you are almost certainly worse off.

Here is what the price tables actually show, and which network makes more sense for the way you buy data.

The Monthly Bundle War: Airtel Lost Its Edge

For years, the simple advice in Kenya was that Airtel gives you more data for the same money. That advice is now outdated.

Safaricom launched its Pata More campaign in August 2026, and the changes were significant. The KES 1,000 monthly bundle moved from 10.2GB to 21GB . The implied cost per gigabyte fell from around KES 100 to roughly KES 47 .

Airtel responded, but not by cutting prices. Its KES 1,000 monthly bundle now offers 25GB . That is still more gigabytes than Safaricom at the same price. But the gap has narrowed dramatically. Before these changes, Airtel's advantage at the monthly tier was enormous. The KES 1,500 Smarta bundle once gave 90GB. It now gives 32GB . The value was cut, not the price.

If you buy monthly and you need maximum gigabytes, Airtel still edges ahead. 25GB against Safaricom's 21GB is a real difference. But it is no longer the landslide it used to be.

The Daily Bundle Trap: Where Small Buyers Got Hurt

This is the part that matters most for boda riders, students, traders, and anyone who buys data in small amounts because that is what the day allows.

Safaricom's B-Live bundles were the go-to for time-based browsing. KES 20 for an hour, KES 50 for three hours, KES 150 for six hours. Those are gone. What remains is a single B-Live offering at KES 25, available only between 23:00 and 16:00 . The hours between 16:00 and 23:00, the exact window when most people finish work and go online, are not covered.

The effective cost of an afternoon online rose from KES 16.67 an hour to KES 25, an increase of 50 per cent . Nobody raised the price of a bundle. They simply removed the bundle you used to buy.

Airtel made a similar move. Its hourly bundle at KES 15 now gives you 1GB only between 23:00 and 15:59. Outside that window, the same KES 15 buys 250MB . The bundle that once gave you a full gigabyte for an hour now gives you a quarter of that during the hours most people are awake.

The Communications Authority estimates Kenyan subscribers use about 5GB a month on average . That average hides a split. Roughly half of Safaricom's data subscribers use less than 1GB in a month . For that half, the hourly bundle was not a luxury. It was the only way to get online at all. Removing it is not a pricing adjustment. It is removing the entry point.

What You Actually Pay Today

For daily buyers, the comparison at the low end is close. At KES 20, both networks give roughly 300MB . At KES 50, Safaricom gives 900MB and Airtel gives 1GB . At KES 99, Safaricom offers 1.8GB and Airtel offers 2.4GB . Airtel wins these comparisons, but the margins are thin.

For monthly buyers, the picture is more nuanced. Airtel's KES 1,000 gives 25GB. Safaricom's KES 1,000 gives 21GB . If gigabytes are your only metric, Airtel wins. But if you also value network coverage outside Nairobi, the calculation changes. Safaricom's coverage in rural areas and along highways remains superior, and that is why many Kenyans keep a Safaricom line even when Airtel is cheaper .

The Verdict

Stay with Safaricom if: you travel outside major towns regularly, you need reliable coverage for M-Pesa and calls, and you buy monthly bundles where the 21GB at KES 1,000 is enough for your needs. The loss of B-Live hurts, but the network reliability is the trade-off you are accepting.

Switch to Airtel if: you are a city-based heavy data user who buys monthly, you want 25GB instead of 21GB at KES 1,000, and you do not need Safaricom's rural coverage. For daily buyers, Airtel's slight edge at KES 50 and KES 99 is real but not transformative.

The truth nobody says out loud: neither network is on your side. Safaricom cut the value of small bundles while boosting the monthly tier, protecting its high-value customers. Airtel quietly reduced the gigabytes in its monthly bundles while keeping the price the same, then offered a slightly better monthly deal to look competitive. The only clear winner in this round is the buyer who can afford to pay KES 1,000 upfront. Everyone else is paying more for less, and the price tag never changed.

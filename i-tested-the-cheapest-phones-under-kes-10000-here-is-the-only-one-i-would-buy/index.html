I Tested the Cheapest Phones Under KES 10,000. Here Is the Only One I Would Buy

Editorial October 4, 2026 Buying Guides

I spent the last two weeks walking into phone shops along Moi Avenue and scrolling through Jumia listings, looking for smartphones that actually cost less than KES 10,000. Not the ones advertised at KES 9,999 before "shipping and taxes." The real ones.

What I found was uncomfortable. Most of the phones in this bracket are not worth your money. They are slow, they run old software, and they will frustrate you within a month. But there is one exception. One phone that does the basics without making you feel like you settled for scrap.

This is not a list of every cheap phone in Kenya. This is what I found after testing the ones that matter.

What KES 10,000 Actually Buys You in 2026

Before I name names, let me be honest about what this price bracket is. A KES 10,000 smartphone in Kenya today is a device for WhatsApp, M-Pesa, calls, and light browsing. That is it. If you expect to play games, take good photos at night, or switch between five apps without them reloading, you are looking at the wrong budget.

The good news is that the basics have improved. Phones at this price now come with 4GB of RAM, 128GB of storage, and Android 15 in some cases . Two years ago, that was unheard of. The bad news is that most of these phones still cut corners where it hurts: screen quality, charging speed, and long-term software support.

The Phones I Tested

I focused on the models that Kenyan buyers are actually considering at this price. That meant the Itel A90, the Tecno Pop 10, the Xiaomi Redmi A5, and the Samsung Galaxy A06 . I also looked at the Itel A06, which is widely available on Jumia at around KES 8,800 .

I did not test feature phones. If you want a Kabambe for calls and SMS only, that is a different conversation. This is about smartphones that can handle M-Pesa, WhatsApp, and a browser without dying.

The One I Would Actually Buy: Itel A90

The Itel A90 is the only phone in this bracket that I can recommend without a list of caveats. At around KES 8,888, it gives you 4GB of RAM and 128GB of storage . That combination is the reason it wins.

Here is why that matters. Most phones at this price give you 2GB or 3GB of RAM. That is not enough for modern Android. WhatsApp alone has become heavier, and M-Pesa's app is not light either. With 4GB, the A90 can keep WhatsApp open while you switch to M-Pesa and back without reloading. That sounds like a small thing until you are standing at a shop counter waiting for your phone to catch up.

The 128GB of storage means you are not deleting photos every week. You can keep your WhatsApp media, your music, and a few videos without worrying about space. The dedicated microSD slot, if you need it, is there.

The battery is 5,000mAh, which got me through a full day of moderate use without a charge. The software is Android 15, which is current for this price bracket . Itel's Carlcare service network is also present in Kenya, which matters more than people admit when something goes wrong .

The A90 is not exciting. The screen is not the sharpest. The camera is basic. But it does the job it was built for, and it does it without making you angry.

Why I Would Skip the Others

The Tecno Pop 10 is the closest competitor. At around KES 9,500 to KES 9,900, it offers a 120Hz display and an IP54 rating for dust and splash resistance . On paper, that is attractive. But the Pop 10 comes with 3GB of RAM in the configuration most shops sell . That extra 1GB of RAM in the Itel is worth more in daily use than a smoother scrolling screen. The 120Hz display is nice. The RAM is necessary.

The Xiaomi Redmi A5 has the cleanest software and the best warranty in this bracket, with a 24+1 month promise . It also has a large 6.88-inch 120Hz display and a 5,200mAh battery . But the version that lands under KES 10,000 typically has 3GB of RAM . If you can stretch to the 4GB variant, it becomes a serious contender. At the base price, the Itel wins on memory.

The Samsung Galaxy A06 is the brand-name option. It has a 50MP camera and Samsung's promise of software updates . But it is old, and at this price, you are paying for the badge more than the experience. The A06 is fine for someone who refuses to use anything other than Samsung. For everyone else, the Itel gives you more phone for less money.

The Itel A06 at KES 8,800 looks tempting, but the Jumia listing shows a 4,000mAh battery and 2GB of RAM in the base configuration, despite conflicting information in the product description . That is a step down from the A90 in the areas that matter most. Do not confuse the A06 with the A90. They are different phones with different capabilities.

The Verdict

Buy the Itel A90 if: your budget is firmly under KES 10,000, you need a phone for WhatsApp, M-Pesa, calls, and light browsing, and you want the most RAM and storage your money can buy. It is the practical choice, not the exciting one.

Skip it if: you can stretch to KES 12,000 or KES 13,000. At that point, the Redmi A5 with 4GB RAM, or even the Redmi 15C we reviewed earlier, becomes a much better long-term purchase. The A90 is the best phone under 10K, but it is still a phone under 10K.

The cheapest phones in Kenya are mostly a trap. The Itel A90 is the one that is not.

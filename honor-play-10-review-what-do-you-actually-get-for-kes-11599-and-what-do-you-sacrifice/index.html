Honor Play 10 Review: What Do You Actually Get for KES 11,599 (And What Do You Sacrifice)?

Editorial October 15, 2026 Reviews

The Honor Play 10 sits in a strange spot in the Kenyan market. At KES 11,599 on Jumia, it undercuts almost everything else in the entry-level bracket, including the Redmi 15C and the Samsung Galaxy A07 we have already examined . For a phone with a 6.74-inch screen and a 5,000mAh battery, that price looks almost too good.

But Honor has made this phone by cutting corners in specific, deliberate places. Some of those cuts are acceptable for a budget buyer. Others will affect how you use the phone every single day. This review is about identifying exactly which is which, before you spend your money.

What KES 11,599 Actually Gets You

The Honor Play 10 ships with 3GB of RAM and 64GB of internal storage . That is a modest configuration, and you should read the 3GB figure carefully. This is not a phone for heavy multitasking.

The display is a 6.74-inch TFT LCD with a resolution of 720 x 1600 pixels . It is a large screen, which is genuinely useful for watching videos or reading on WhatsApp. Honor lists a 90Hz refresh rate for this panel, which should make scrolling feel smoother than a standard 60Hz screen .

The processor is a MediaTek Helio G81, an entry-level chip that Honor pairs with Android 15 Go Edition . Android Go is a stripped-down version of Android designed for phones with limited memory. That software choice tells you everything about the phone's ambitions.

The battery is 5,000mAh, and charging is listed at 10W through a USB-C port . The main camera is 13MP, and the selfie camera is 5MP . You get a side-mounted fingerprint scanner, a 3.5mm headphone jack, and FM radio support . There is no NFC, so you cannot use it for tap-to-pay services .

The Sacrifices You Will Feel Every Day

The first sacrifice is performance. The Helio G81 paired with 3GB of RAM and Android Go is not built for anything beyond basic tasks. Calls, WhatsApp, M-Pesa, and light browsing will work. But if you try to switch between several apps, or use anything that demands sustained processing power, the phone will slow down. A benchmark score for the Play 10 shows a 3DMark Wild Life result of 644, with graphics tests running at roughly 4 frames per second . That number is low. It confirms that gaming is not part of this phone's job description.

The second sacrifice is the screen quality. A 720p resolution on a 6.74-inch display means a pixel density of around 260 pixels per inch . Text will look soft. Images will lack sharpness. This is the same compromise we saw on the Redmi 15C and the Galaxy A07, and it is the standard trade-off at this price. But on a display this large, the softness is more noticeable than it would be on a smaller screen.

The third sacrifice is charging speed. The 5,000mAh battery is a healthy capacity, but 10W charging is slow by any standard . If you drain the phone completely, you should expect to leave it plugged in for a significant portion of the day before it is full. Some Jumia listings for this phone bundle a power bank as a free gift . That inclusion is not generosity. It is an admission that the charging speed is a problem.

Where the Play 10 Gets It Right

The price is the obvious win. At KES 11,599, this phone costs less than the Redmi 15C and the Galaxy A07. If your budget is hard-capped below KES 12,000, the Play 10 is one of the few options that does not force you to compromise on screen size or battery capacity.

The storage situation is better than the RAM situation. You get 64GB internally, and the phone supports microSD cards up to 1TB through a dedicated slot . That means you can carry two SIM cards and still expand storage. For anyone who keeps offline videos or music, this is a practical advantage.

The 3.5mm headphone jack and FM radio are small features that matter in Kenya. Wired earphones are cheap and reliable, and FM radio does not consume data. Honor has kept both, and budget buyers should appreciate that.

The Verdict

Buy the Honor Play 10 if: your absolute ceiling is around KES 12,000, you need a large screen for basic media consumption, and you accept that this phone is for calls, messaging, and light browsing only. It is a first smartphone, a backup phone, or a device for someone who does not demand performance.

Skip it if: you can stretch to the Redmi 15C or the Galaxy A07. Both offer more RAM, better processors, and more usable daily performance. The Play 10 saves you a few hundred shillings, but the 3GB of RAM and Android Go software will make themselves felt sooner than you expect.

The Honor Play 10 is not a bad phone. It is a phone with a very narrow job description. If your needs fit inside that description, the price is fair. If they do not, the sacrifices will frustrate you long before the battery ever runs flat.

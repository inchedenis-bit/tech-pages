Stop Buying 4GB RAM Laptops: The Minimum Specs Your Money Should Buy in Kenya Today

Editorial September 1, 2026 Buying Guides

Walk into any electronics shop in Nairobi and ask for a laptop under KES 40,000. The seller will proudly hand you a shiny new machine with 4GB of RAM. It looks nice. The box has big pictures on it. And if you buy it, you will regret it within a week.

The problem is not that 4GB laptops are broken. The problem is that modern software has outgrown them. If you are spending your hard-earned money today, you need to know exactly what specs to demand before you pay.

Here is the absolute minimum your money should buy in Kenya right now.

The RAM Rule: 8GB is the Floor

RAM is your laptop's short-term memory. It is what allows you to have WhatsApp open while you are typing an assignment in Microsoft Word and checking a PDF in a browser tab.

If you buy a 4GB laptop, your computer will constantly run out of space to think. It will freeze. Tabs will crash. You will spend more time waiting for the spinning wheel than actually working.

Forget 4GB. Do not buy a laptop with less than 8GB of RAM. If you can stretch to 16GB, do it. It is the single best upgrade you can make for a machine you plan to use for the next three years.

The Storage Rule: Never Buy a Mechanical Hard Drive

This is the biggest trick in the budget laptop market. You will see a cheap laptop advertised with "1TB Storage!" and think it is a great deal.

What they do not tell you is that it is a mechanical hard drive (HDD). This is old technology. It is slow, it is fragile, and it makes starting your computer feel like a chore.

You want an SSD (Solid State Drive). It has no moving parts. It boots Windows in seconds instead of minutes. A 256GB SSD will feel ten times faster than a 1TB HDD.

The minimum spec: 256GB SSD. Do not accept an HDD, no matter how big the storage number is.

The Processor Rule: Ignore the Speed, Check the Generation

Processors are confusing. You will see laptops advertised as "Intel Core i5" or "Core i7" and assume they are fast. But an old i7 from 2016 will be slower than a newer i3 from 2021.

If you are buying a new budget laptop, look for at least an Intel Core i3 (11th Gen or newer) or an AMD Ryzen 3 (4000 series or newer).

If you are buying a refurbished business laptop, look for at least an 8th Gen Intel Core i5. This is the sweet spot for performance and price in Kenya right now.

The Screen Rule: Full HD is Not Optional

Many cheap laptops come with a 1366 x 768 screen. This is not High Definition. Text looks blurry. Images look washed out. After two hours of reading, your eyes will hurt.

You must demand a 1080p (Full HD) screen. It is the standard for modern computing. If a seller cannot confirm the screen is Full HD, walk away.

The One Thing You Can Ignore

You will see laptops advertised with "Dedicated Graphics" or "NVIDIA GeForce" for KES 45,000.

Unless you are buying a laptop specifically to play heavy video games or render 3D animation, ignore this. A modern integrated graphics chip is perfectly fine for watching YouTube, using Zoom, and running normal university software. Do not let a seller charge you extra for graphics you do not need.

The Verdict

When you go shopping in Nairobi today, write this list down and do not compromise:

  • RAM: 8GB minimum
  • Storage: 256GB SSD minimum
  • Processor: 11th Gen Intel i3 / Ryzen 3, or 8th Gen Intel i5 (refurbished)
  • Screen: Full HD (1080p)

If a laptop does not meet these minimums, do not buy it. Save your money, look for a refurbished business machine, or wait for a better deal. Buying a slow laptop today is throwing money away.

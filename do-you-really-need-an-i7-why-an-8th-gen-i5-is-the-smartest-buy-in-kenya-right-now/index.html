Do You Really Need an i7? Why an 8th-Gen i5 Is the Smartest Buy in Kenya Right Now

Lina Maya September 10, 2026 Buying Guides

When you walk into a laptop shop in Nairobi, the seller will often try to upsell you. They will point to a machine and say, "This one has an Intel Core i7." They say it with reverence, as if the i7 badge magically makes the laptop faster and better.

Because of this, many Kenyan buyers stretch their budgets to the breaking point just to get an i7 sticker on their laptop. But here is a secret that tech sellers do not always explain: an older i7 is often a terrible deal.

If you are shopping for a laptop today, the smartest money you can spend is on a machine with an 8th-Generation Intel Core i5. Here is why.

The Myth of the "i7"

The first thing to understand is that "i3," "i5," and "i7" are just tiers. They tell you where the processor sits in Intel's lineup for that specific year. They do not tell you how old the processor is, or how fast it actually runs.

A 2014 Intel Core i7 is much, much slower than a 2020 Intel Core i3.

Because refurbished laptops are old, the market is flooded with old i7 chips. If you buy a "cheap" i7 laptop for KES 35,000, you might actually be buying a 6th-Generation or 7th-Generation chip from 2016. It has the fancy badge, but it lacks the modern architecture, the power efficiency, and the security features of newer chips.

The Sweet Spot: 8th-Gen i5

The 8th-Generation Intel Core i5 is the perfect middle ground for Kenyan buyers right now. Here is why it makes sense:

1. It is powerful enough for everything you do.
An 8th-gen i5 has four cores and eight threads. It can handle Microsoft Office, dozens of browser tabs, Zoom calls, and even light coding or design work without breaking a sweat. You do not need an i7 for standard university or office work.

2. It is cheap enough to fit your budget.
Because it is a few years old, the 8th-gen i5 is widely available in the refurbished market. You can find excellent HP EliteBooks and Lenovo ThinkPads with this chip for between KES 28,000 and KES 38,000.

3. It runs Windows 11 perfectly.
This is the most important point. Microsoft requires laptops to have an 8th-Generation Intel processor (or newer) to officially run Windows 11. If you buy a 7th-Gen i7, you are stuck on Windows 10, which is losing support. An 8th-Gen i5 guarantees you can run the modern operating system smoothly.

When Should You Actually Buy an i7?

An i7 is not useless. But you only need it if you are doing heavy, demanding tasks.

If you are a video editor rendering 4K footage, a 3D animator, or a software developer running multiple virtual machines, an i7 will save you time. You will feel the difference.

But if you are a student writing essays, a business owner doing accounts, or someone browsing the web, an i7 is a waste of money. You will never push the processor hard enough to notice the difference, but you will definitely notice the lighter wallet.

How to Spot a Good Deal

When you look at a refurbished laptop, do not just read the sticker. Ask the seller for the exact model number of the processor.

If it is an i5, it should end with a "G" number between 8000 and 8999 (like the i5-8250U or i5-8350U). That means it is an 8th-Gen chip.

If the seller shows you an i7, check the number. If it is an i7-7500U, it is a 7th-Gen chip. It is older, and it cannot officially run Windows 11. Walk away.

The Verdict

Do not get fooled by the badge. The letter on the sticker matters less than the generation number next to it.

For the vast majority of Kenyan buyers, an 8th-Gen Intel Core i5 is the smartest, safest, and most cost-effective choice. It gives you the speed you need, it runs the software you want, and it leaves money in your pocket for an SSD upgrade or a new battery.

Save the i7 for the video editors. Buy the i5, and use the savings to buy a laptop sleeve.

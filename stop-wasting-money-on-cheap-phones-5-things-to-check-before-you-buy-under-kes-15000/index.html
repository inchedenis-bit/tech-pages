Stop Wasting Money on “Cheap” Phones: 5 Things to Check Before You Buy Under KES 15,000

Editorial September 28, 2026 Buying Guides

The cheapest phone in the shop is rarely the cheapest phone to own. A KES 9,000 device that makes you wait for WhatsApp to open every morning, or dies after eighteen months because the software was abandoned, has cost you more than the extra KES 4,000 you refused to spend.

I have reviewed enough budget phones in Kenya to see the same mistakes repeated. Buyers focus on the price tag and the camera megapixels. They ignore the five things that actually determine whether a phone will still be usable in 2029. Here they are, in order of importance.

1. RAM: 4GB Is the Floor, Not the Target

Most budget phones in Kenya advertise 4GB of RAM. Some still sell 3GB or even 2GB. Do not buy anything below 4GB.

The reason is simple. Android itself, WhatsApp, M-Pesa, and a browser will consume almost all of 4GB. If you drop to 3GB, apps will reload every time you switch between them. You will stand at a shop counter waiting for M-Pesa to restart because you checked a WhatsApp message thirty seconds earlier. That is not a minor inconvenience. It is a daily tax on your time.

If you can stretch to 6GB, do it. A 6GB phone will keep more apps alive in the background, and it will feel less strained two years from now. The Samsung Galaxy A07 and the Infinix Hot 60i both offer variants with more memory, and the difference is felt immediately .

2. Storage: 128GB Is the Minimum for a Phone You Keep

A 64GB phone sounds adequate until you account for the system. Android and pre-installed apps occupy 20 to 30GB before you have taken a single photo . WhatsApp cache alone can grow to 15GB over months of normal use. That leaves you with a phone that is constantly warning you about low storage.

The budget bracket has largely moved to 128GB, and you should insist on it. The Xiaomi Redmi 15C and the Infinix Hot 60i both offer 128GB configurations in this price range . If a seller offers only 64GB at a tempting price, walk away. You will spend the next two years deleting photos and clearing app data.

3. Software Updates: The Number Nobody Asks About

This is the single most important spec that phone shops never mention. A phone without security updates is a phone that will eventually stop running modern banking apps. It is also a phone that becomes a security risk.

Samsung is the clear leader here. The Galaxy A07 is promised six major Android upgrades and six years of security patches . No other brand in this price bracket matches that commitment. Tecno and Infinix have improved, promising up to three OS upgrades on 2026 models, but that is still half of what Samsung offers .

If you plan to keep your phone for three years or more, the update promise should carry more weight than a slightly better camera or a slightly larger battery. A phone that still receives security patches in 2030 is worth more than one that does not.

4. The Charger in the Box: Check Before You Pay

Some brands no longer include a charger. Others include a slow one while advertising fast charging. This is not a minor detail. A 6,000mAh battery charged at 10W takes hours to fill. The same battery charged at 33W is usable within an hour.

Jumia listings for budget phones are inconsistent on this point. Some show the charger clearly. Others do not mention it at all. Before you pay, confirm with the seller what is actually in the box. If the charger is missing, add the cost of buying one to the price of the phone. That “cheap” deal may no longer look cheap.

5. Buy from a Seller Who Will Still Be There Next Year

Counterfeit phones remain a real problem in Kenya, particularly in street markets and from unverified online sellers . A fake phone reports inflated specs in Settings, lacks proper type approval, and often fails within months.

There are two checks you can do before handing over money. First, dial #06# and confirm the IMEI matches the number printed on the box and etched into the SIM tray . Second, buy from an authorized retailer. Jumia’s official stores, brand shops, and established physical retailers offer warranties and return policies. A seller who cannot offer a receipt with the IMEI is a seller you should not trust.

Tecno, Infinix, and itel service through Carlcare centres nationwide. Samsung and Xiaomi have their own authorized networks . A warranty is only useful if there is somewhere to claim it.

The Rule That Ties It All Together

A budget phone is not a disposable purchase. It is a tool you will rely on for banking, communication, and work for years. The five checks above are not about getting the best specs. They are about avoiding the phones that will fail you quietly, one slow app launch at a time.

Spend the extra KES 2,000 for 128GB and 6GB of RAM if you can. Choose the brand that promises updates over the brand that promises megapixels. Confirm the charger. Buy from someone accountable.

The cheapest phone is the one you do not have to replace in eighteen months.

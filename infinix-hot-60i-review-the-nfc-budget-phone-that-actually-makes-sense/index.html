Infinix Hot 60i Review: The NFC Budget Phone That Actually Makes Sense

Editorial October 10, 2026 Reviews

NFC is one of those features that budget phones usually skip. It is expensive to licence, rarely advertised in this price bracket, and most buyers do not know they want it until they try to use it. The Infinix Hot 60i includes it anyway, and in Kenya in 2026, that decision looks smarter than Infinix probably intended.

Safaricom and Pesapal have been rolling out Tap-to-Pay across merchant outlets in Kenya, allowing customers with NFC-enabled phones to tap on a POS terminal and complete an M-Pesa payment without entering a Till number . This is not a future concept. It is live at Java House, Artcaffé, and thousands of other retail outlets already using Pesapal terminals . A budget phone with NFC is no longer a novelty. It is a practical advantage.

But NFC alone does not make a phone worth buying. The Hot 60i has real strengths and real compromises. Here is what you actually get.

What the KES 14,899 Configuration Offers

The Hot 60i is sold in several configurations in Kenya, and the pricing is inconsistent. Jumia lists a 6GB/128GB model at KES 14,899 , while another listing shows an 8GB/256GB variant at KES 19,750 . A separate seller lists a 6GB/128GB unit at KES 23,500, which is clearly overpriced for this bracket . For the sub-15K buyer, the Jumia 6GB/128GB listing is the relevant model.

The processor is a MediaTek Helio G81 Ultimate, paired with Android 15 and Infinix's XOS 15.1 skin . The battery is 5,160mAh, and some listings claim 45W fast charging, though this figure varies by region and seller . The display is a 6.7-inch panel, and here the listings disagree sharply. Some sources list it as 720p , while one Jumia listing claims 1080x2460 FHD+ . You should confirm the resolution with the seller before paying, because the difference is significant.

The camera setup is a 50MP main sensor paired with a 2MP secondary lens, and an 8MP selfie camera . There is a side-mounted fingerprint sensor, a 3.5mm headphone jack, and stereo speakers . The microSD situation is also unclear across listings, so verify expandability if you need it.

The NFC Advantage in Kenya

This is the headline feature, and it deserves proper context.

M-Pesa Tap-to-Pay works by tapping an NFC-enabled phone on an NFC-enabled POS terminal, verifying the transaction details, and entering your M-Pesa PIN . It reduces queues, cuts down on wrong Till number entries, and speeds up checkout. For a Kenyan buyer who frequents supermarkets, coffee shops, or any merchant with a Pesapal terminal, this is a genuinely useful feature that most sub-15K phones do not offer.

The catch is that Tap-to-Pay is not universal yet. It works only at merchants with enabled POS devices, and the rollout is expanding but not complete . If you shop mostly at informal traders, kiosks, or matatu stages, you will not use NFC often. But if you live in Nairobi and pay at established retailers, the convenience is real.

The Compromises You Will Feel

The display is the biggest concern. If the Kenyan units are indeed 720p, text and images will look soft on a 6.7-inch screen. This is the standard trade-off in this bracket, but it is worth confirming before purchase because the listing information is contradictory.

The second compromise is storage speed. The Hot 60i uses eMMC 5.1 storage, which is slower than the UFS technology found in slightly more expensive phones . App launches and file transfers will feel slower than they should, especially if you are used to a faster device.

The third compromise is performance. The Helio G81 Ultimate is an entry-level chip. Benchmark results show a 3DMark Wild Life score of 648, with graphics tests running at roughly 4 frames per second . WhatsApp, M-Pesa, and browsing will work. Gaming and heavy multitasking will not. This is a phone for the basics.

The Verdict

Buy the Infinix Hot 60i if: you want NFC for M-Pesa Tap-to-Pay at a budget price, you need a large screen and a reliable battery for daily use, and you accept that this is a device for communication and light browsing rather than performance. The 6GB/128GB model at KES 14,899 is the version to target.

Skip it if: you can stretch to the Samsung Galaxy A07 for its six-year update promise, you need a confirmed sharp display and cannot verify the resolution before buying, or you rarely shop at merchants with NFC terminals. In those cases, the NFC advantage disappears, and the Hot 60i becomes an ordinary budget phone with an uncertain spec sheet.

The Infinix Hot 60i is not the best phone under KES 15,000 on raw performance. But it is the only one that anticipates how Kenyans will pay for things in the next few years. That makes it worth considering, provided you confirm exactly what you are buying.

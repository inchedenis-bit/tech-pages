HP vs. Lenovo: Which Refurbished Laptop Brand Actually Deserves Your KES 30,000?

Editorial September 4, 2026 Comparisons

If you walk into any laptop shop in Nairobi CBD with KES 30,000, you will be shown two main options. The seller will either hand you an HP EliteBook or a Lenovo ThinkPad.

Both are business laptops. Both were originally bought by big companies abroad, used for a few years, and shipped here. Both will give you far better performance than a brand-new laptop at the same price.

But they are not the same machine. They feel different, they are built differently, and they break in different ways. If you have KES 30,000 to spend, here is the honest breakdown of which one you should actually buy.

The Keyboard: Lenovo Wins

If you are a student writing long essays or a freelancer typing all day, this is the most important feature on the laptop.

The Lenovo ThinkPad has the best keyboard in the business. The keys have a deep, satisfying click. They are slightly curved to fit your fingertips. Once you type on a ThinkPad, typing on anything else feels like a downgrade.

The HP EliteBook keyboard is good, but it is not great. It is quieter, flatter, and less satisfying to use for long hours.

Winner: Lenovo ThinkPad.

The Build Quality: HP Wins

HP EliteBooks are built like tanks. They are made of aluminium and magnesium. If you drop it in your backpack or bump it against a desk, it will survive.

Lenovo ThinkPads are also durable, but they use more plastic in their construction. They are tough, but they do not feel as premium in the hand as the HP.

If you commute on matatus every day and your laptop gets tossed around, the HP gives you a little more peace of mind.

Winner: HP EliteBook.

The Screen: HP Wins

When you buy a refurbished laptop, you are usually looking at a 14-inch screen. HP generally uses better panels on the EliteBook line. The colours are slightly richer, and the viewing angles are wider.

The ThinkPad screens are perfectly fine for typing and reading, but they can look a bit dull. If you plan to watch a lot of YouTube or movies on your laptop, the HP looks better.

Winner: HP EliteBook.

The Repairability: Lenovo Wins

When a laptop breaks, you want it fixed fast and cheap.

Lenovo ThinkPads are famously easy to repair. You can remove the back cover with a few screws, swap the battery, upgrade the RAM, or change the SSD in minutes. Parts are everywhere in Nairobi.

HP EliteBooks are also repairable, but they are slightly more complicated to open up and the parts can sometimes be a little harder to find in local shops.

Winner: Lenovo ThinkPad.

The Battery Life: A Tie

Both brands suffer from the same problem: they are refurbished. That means the battery has already been used for a few years.

Whether you buy an HP or a Lenovo, you should expect about three to four hours of battery life. If the seller promises eight hours, they are lying. You will likely need to replace the battery within your first year of ownership on either machine.

Winner: Tie.

The Verdict

Both laptops are excellent choices for KES 30,000. You cannot go wrong with either one, but your choice depends on what you value most.

Buy the Lenovo ThinkPad if: you type a lot, you want the best keyboard, and you like the idea of easily upgrading the RAM or storage yourself.

Buy the HP EliteBook if: you want the toughest build, the best screen, and a machine that feels more premium when you open it up in a lecture hall.

If you are a computer science student who spends all day coding, get the Lenovo. If you are a business student who watches a lot of video lectures, get the HP.
